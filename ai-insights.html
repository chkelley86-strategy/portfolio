AI Insights: moving users past data to meaning

We were giving users more data, but not helping them make meaningful decisions. I designed HubSpot's first AI product: plain-language insights that summarize a report, highlight what matters, and connect it to action.

Where AI shows up

  • Generative summaries: natural-language insights generated for each report through HubSpot's generative AI wrapper.
  • Prompt design: shaping what the model highlights so insights read like a good analyst, not a data dump.
  • A foundation for more: forecasting, anomaly detection and generated report descriptions followed.

The problem

Reporting kept adding data and features, but users still struggled to work out what was meaningful and what to do about it.

"Is this data or information? What is meaningful, how do I make something meaningful…"Marketing director, HubSpot customer

Two kinds of users, one gap

ConsumersProducers
Know the business but not their data. Need to know what's meaningful so they can act on it.

"It's hard to pull meaningful information from the dashboard."		 Spend more time with data and need to explain why it matters to decision makers.

"I have to send along video or a text box and I'm like why is this so much work?"

The approach: curate, don't dump

The goal was to take noisy data and do three things a good analyst would: summarize complex information, highlight what's salient, and connect information across reports.

I moved from customer touchpoints to lo-fi concepts, tested with a small set of customers, then validated at scale. Early users wanted insights that were natural-language, automatic, easy to configure, connected to the rest of reporting, and paired with visuals.

Prioritizing with a Kano survey

We had many possible directions, so I ran a Kano survey testing 14 features with 105 users to learn what they expected, what would delight them, and what they didn't want.

  • Prioritize: users expect more advanced breakdowns and groupings of their reports.
  • Aspire: users want information that goes beyond their own reading of a chart.
  • Rethink: none of the tested features that suggested actions or resources were desirable. Users wanted insight, not instructions.

That last finding shaped the product. AI Insights curates and explains, and it leaves the decision to the user.

Scoping the MVP

To ship fast with existing models, we focused on one report at a time and generated summary text through HubSpot's generative AI wrapper. We left out a chat interface, customization, generated visuals and modularity.

Iterating on the experience

The first design showed insights in a modal. It caused technical issues, and in testing users preferred seeing all insights at once, next to the chart they described.

"This is exactly what I would be looking for… just, really quick, interesting insights that I can share out."Usability test participant

Impact

AI Insights was activated by 185k users, and average monthly usage rose 25% after activation. As HubSpot's first AI product, it paved the way for fast follows: forecasting, anomaly detection and generated report descriptions. Those display options were adopted by more than 100k users and improved two-week retention by 30%.

What I learned

  • Prompt engineering is design work. The quality of an insight depends as much on the prompt and its framing as on the UI around it.
  • Evidence tells you where to stop. The Kano survey kept us from building recommendations users didn't want.
  • Ship the slice, then connect it. Next steps were linking insights to drill-throughs and an AI assistant.
