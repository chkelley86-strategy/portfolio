Work / 01 / HubSpot

Lead Scoring: rebuilt around AI, so marketers start from data instead of a blank page

Two reusable AI components recommend the rules that actually convert and explain each rule's impact. After launch, 17% more scores were created, and 30k users have built 25k scores.

Role
Lead product designer
Team
PM, front-end and back-end tech leads, design
Timeframe
2025–2026
Focus
AI recommendations, platform components
Problem

Marketers guessed which signals mattered, tested scores by hand, and took more than two hours to build a first score.

What I did

Led design for an AI-first rebuild: AI rule discovery, rule insights, and a modular builder users can enter from anywhere.

Result

+17% more scores created. 25k scores built by 30k users in the new app.

00 · Why it mattered

A marketer wants to know whether her new lead score works. So she opens her own marketing emails, clicks the links, and watches her contact record to see whether the points go up.

That was a real workaround from our research. It showed the core problem: HubSpot had all the data to tell her which signals mattered, but nothing in the product surfaced it.

Business need

AI turned the buyer journey into an hourglass: fewer leads arrive, but they arrive ready to buy. Competitors were marketing instant setup and AI-built models. HubSpot's advantage was native data across the whole customer journey, but users couldn't get to it.

Target: a first score in under 30 minutes, down from 2+ hours.

User need

Marketers needed to know which signals actually lead to customers and whether their score worked. Sales needed to see why a lead was prioritized so they could act on it.

"You need like a PhD in HubSpot to get things going."
01 · What the research showed

The builder wasn't where scoring failed. The months around it were.

I mapped the full scoring journey with seven customers, from planning a score to refining it months later, instead of studying only the builder screen.

01

The cold start

A blank builder and hundreds of properties. Users had no idea which few rules would move the needle.

02

The knowledge plateau

Existing users stuck to obvious rules and never found high-converting signals outside their assumptions.

03

Testing by hand

Nothing showed whether a score worked, so users clicked their own emails to check.

02 · Key decisions

Reusable AI components, not a single linear flow.

The old app was one long form you filled in top to bottom. Instead, we designed reusable AI components that can be called from different parts of the experience.

Decision 01

Start from AI-ranked rules, not an empty page

Problem
New users landed on an empty page with hundreds of properties to choose from.
Evidence
The cold start was the first place users stalled. Building a first score took more than two hours.
Solution
A new empty state with three ways in, led by Discover AI rules: every possible rule for the chosen goal, ranked by reach, conversions and conversion rate, each with a confidence label.
Decision 02

Build components, not a wizard

Problem
A linear setup flow only helped on day one. Existing users plateaued on obvious rules.
Evidence
Users came back months later, or dug into one rule and wanted to find better ones.
Solution
Made Discover AI rules a standalone component. The same ranked table opens from a blank score and from any rule's insights, filtered to similar high-converting rules.
Decision 03 · The pivot

Show each rule's impact right where users work

Problem
Nothing showed whether a rule was doing anything, so users tested scores by hand.
Evidence
Users clicked their own emails to check scores, and wanted to verify their model before turning it on.
Solution
Rule insights for every rule: how many leads it reaches, its impact on the overall score, and the average point distribution, explained in plain language.

What changedThe first design put insights in a panel on the right side of the builder. After user feedback and technical limits, insights moved inline, directly under the rule they describe, so users never lose their place.

What I learned

Map the whole job, not the screen.

The builder wasn't where scoring failed. The handoff and the months after were.

Confidence is a design material.

Users didn't need more rules. They needed to see that their rules worked.

Components outlast flows.

Building Discover and Insights as reusable pieces let one design serve new and returning users.
